### Source code and project writings are speech

The source, documentation, comments, design writings, and commit messages of GreenJade are expressive work. They are protected as authorship under copyright law and, as against the government, as speech.

- *Bernstein v. U.S. Dep’t of State*, 922 F. Supp. 1426 (N.D. Cal. 1996), and *Bernstein v. U.S. Dep’t of Justice*, 176 F.3d 1132 (9th Cir. 1999) (opinion withdrawn pending rehearing en banc; the district-court holding that source code is speech was not repudiated as a principle): encryption source code is expression; a prepublication licensing scheme on it is a prior restraint.
- *Junger v. Daley*, 209 F.3d 481 (6th Cir. 2000): source code is “an expressive means for the exchange of information and ideas about computer programming” and is protected by the First Amendment.
- *Universal City Studios, Inc. v. Corley*, 273 F.3d 429 (2d Cir. 2001): computer code is speech. Functional aspects may still be regulated under intermediate scrutiny. Coverage is not immunity from every statute.

Compelled speech and prior restraint remain forbidden. *W. Va. State Bd. of Educ. v. Barnette*, 319 U.S. 624 (1943); *Wooley v. Maynard*, 430 U.S. 705 (1977); *303 Creative LLC v. Elenis*, 600 U.S. 570 (2023); *N.Y. Times Co. v. United States*, 403 U.S. 713 (1971).

### Age-gates, identity mandates, and operating-system signal laws

*Free Speech Coalition, Inc. v. Paxton*, 606 U.S. ___ (2025), upheld a Texas statute requiring commercial websites whose inventory is sexual material harmful to minors to verify age. That holding is limited to that class of sites. It is not a holding that a kernel tree, its design writings, or a public source repository may be forced to implement government-ID or age-assurance machinery.

This project, as published, is source, documentation, and engineering artifacts. It is not a commercial site of material obscene as to minors. It is not a covered application store.

Several states have enacted or proposed two different kinds of rules:

1. **App-store accountability acts** (including Texas S.B. 2420, and similar acts in Utah, Louisiana, and Alabama) impose age verification and parental-consent duties on app-store operators and on developers who ship through those stores.
2. **Operating-system signal acts** (including California’s Digital Age Assurance Act, Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1798.500 et seq. (A.B. 1043, effective 1 January 2027); Illinois P.A. 104-0664 (effective 1 January 2028); and Colorado S.B. 26-051 (effective 1 July 2028)) require an “operating system provider” to collect age or date of birth at account setup and to emit an age-bracket signal to applications.

California defines “operating system provider” as a person or entity that “develops, licenses, or controls the operating system software on a computer, mobile device, or any other general purpose computing device.” The author’s position on that definition, as applied to this project, is:

- Publication of a public git repository is not control of operating-system software *on* a device. After a licensee copies the work under MIT or Apache-2.0, the licensee builds and runs that copy. The author does not operate the installed bits.
- The California setup-prompt duty exists “for the purpose of providing a signal … to applications available in a covered application store.” This tree ships no such store.
- The principal author does not reside in California. California law is presumed not to operate extraterritorially unless the Legislature clearly so provides. *Sullivan v. Oracle Corp.*, 51 Cal. 4th 1191 (2011). A State may not project its product-design code onto commerce that takes place wholly outside its borders. *Edgar v. MITE Corp.*, 457 U.S. 624 (1982).
- Forcing the author to write, ship, and maintain a state-prescribed age-collection interface and a state-prescribed age-bracket signal is compelled speech. *Barnette*; *Wooley*; *303 Creative*. The author objects to any construction of A.B. 1043, P.A. 104-0664, or like statutes that treats publication of this source as an order to build that apparatus.

Colorado S.B. 26-051 exempts distribution under license terms that permit copying, redistribution, and modification. California A.B. 1856 would add a similar exclusion; it is not enacted as of the date of this notice. Illinois P.A. 104-0664 contains no open-source exclusion.

Code §§ 1798.99.28–1798.99.40, A.B. 1043, P.A. 104-0664, S.B. 26-051, and any other rule that actually applies to their facts. Piphus*, 435 U.S. 247 (1978); punitive damages under § 1983 require evil motive or reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights, *Smith v.
